## Where the poems come from

I live and write at Braidwood, in the southern tablelands of New South Wales, Australia, on the unceded Country of the Ngunnawal and Yuin peoples. I am a captain in the local Rural Fire Service brigade and turn out when the pager goes. Most of what I write circles back to the same handful of things: the land, the people on it, and what fire and weather do to both. When it strays, it strays towards myths and rebellion, and sometimes both at once.

2026 is the first year I have sent poems out into the world, and they have travelled further than I expected. ‘Bogong’ won the 2026 Venie Holmgren Environmental Poetry Prize; Patrick Jones, one of the two judges, said it “haunts acutely the material before us as moderns, while travels in mythic time, acutely and ethereally.” Two poems, ‘Shearwater’ and ‘Magpies, Year Four’, were shortlisted for the Bridport Prize 2026, and ‘Underburn’ for the Robert Gray Prize for Poetry 2026, and so will be published in the anthology Oystercatcher Three. ‘Hecuba’ is on the longlist for the Winchester Poetry Prize 2026, judged by Julia Copus, and will be published with the winners in October. ‘The singing, not the song’ appears in Cordite Poetry Review.

I write in two registers that keep swapping places: a plain documentary line that comes from radio logs, coronial statements and the shire’s records, and a mythic one in which Penelope or Cassandra find themselves in modern Australia. Longer work in progress includes a verse epic on the Roman civil wars and a play in couplets about Orpheus and Eurydice. My poesis asks of me: precision, and an ear for how words sound to the people who have to hear them.

## On Instagram

I read poems on Instagram ([@rpbuzby](https://www.instagram.com/rpbuzby/)): my own once they are published, and otherwise other people’s, from Clare and Hopkins to Judith Wright and Les Murray, with a few words on why. ‘Bogong’ was the first of mine, read on 6 September 2026.
